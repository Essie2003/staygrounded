A little understanding

You are not the only one who feels this way.

Derealization can make your surroundings feel dreamlike, distant, foggy, or unfamiliar. The sensation can be frightening, especially when you don't know why it's happening.

☁️

It can feel strange

Some people describe the world as hazy, flat, distant, or like they're watching a movie. The feeling itself isn't proof that reality has changed.

🌿

You don't have to fight it

Trying to force the sensation away or constantly checking whether you feel normal can add pressure. Gently return attention to an ordinary activity if that feels manageable.

Tools for right now

You only need to do the next small thing.

Choose one exercise. If focusing on your body or breathing makes you feel worse, stop and try an external grounding activity instead.

STEP 1 OF 5

Come back to your surroundings

A gentle 5–4–3–2–1 sensory exercise. There is no perfect way to do it.

Notice 5 things you can see

Let your eyes rest on ordinary objects around you. Silently name five colours, shapes, or details. No need to rush.

You can keep your eyes open and stay seated. Stop if this exercise feels uncomfortable.

Support beyond this page

You deserve support that meets you where you are.

Grounding tools may help you cope, but you don't have to manage persistent or distressing symptoms alone.

Talk with a qualified professional

If these feelings are frequent, new, worsening, or interfering with sleep, school, work, or relationships, consider speaking with a doctor or licensed mental health professional. They can assess possible causes and discuss appropriate support.

Tell someone you trust

You could say: “I've been feeling disconnected and it scares me sometimes. I don't need you to fix it; could you sit with me or check in on me?”

Care for the basics

Where possible, keep a regular sleep routine, eat and drink regularly, take breaks from overwhelming stimulation, and avoid using alcohol or recreational drugs to manage symptoms.

If you feel unsafe

If you might hurt yourself, cannot keep yourself safe, or have a sudden severe change in awareness, seek urgent local medical help or contact emergency services. Ask someone trusted to stay with you.

A gentle reminder: This website is for education and self-support, not diagnosis or treatment. Derealization can have different causes; a health professional can help you understand what's happening for you. You can stop any exercise that increases distress.
